Part 1

Three types of impurities

Water from rivers and lakes is not pure. It contains impurities — unwanted substances mixed in the water.

Insoluble impurities

Do not dissolve in water.
We can see them.

e.g. sand, mud, dead leaves

Soluble impurities

Dissolve in water.
We cannot see them.

e.g. salt, sugar

Microorganisms

Tiny living things. Too small to see with the naked eye. Some cause diseases.

e.g. bacteria, viruses

Method 1

Sedimentation

1
Muddy water
Muddy water contains insoluble impurities such as sand and mud. The particles are spread all through the water.
2
Leave it to stand
Leave the water to stand for some time. The heavier particles slowly sink and settle at the bottom.
3
The sediment forms
The layer of solid at the bottom is called the sediment. The clearer water on top can be poured off gently.
!
Limitation
Very tiny particles, soluble impurities and microorganisms are still in the water. Sedimentation alone cannot make water pure.

Method 2

Filtration ① — using filter paper

1
Set up
Fold a filter paper into a cone. Put it in a filter funnel on top of a conical flask.
2
Pour the muddy water
Pour the muddy water slowly into the filter paper. Watch what stays on the paper and what drips through.
3
Residue and filtrate
The solid left on the filter paper is the residue.
The clear liquid that passes through is the filtrate.
4
Why does it work?
Filter paper has many tiny holes called pores. Particles bigger than the pores are trapped. Water and very small particles pass through.
mudwaterdissolved saltbacteria
!
Limitation
Soluble impurities and most microorganisms are smaller than the pores, so they pass through. The filtrate is clear, but it is not pure.
Method 2

Filtration ② — using a filter column

1
Layers in the column
A filter column has layers. From top to bottom: gravel (small stones), coarse sand, fine sand and cotton wool.
2
Pour in muddy water
Pour muddy water in at the top. Big particles are trapped first. Smaller particles are trapped lower down, in the sand.
3
Same idea as filter paper
The gaps between stones and sand grains work like the pores of filter paper. The trapped solids are the residue; the water collected is the filtrate.

Real waterworks use huge sand filter beds that work in the same way.

Method 3

Distillation

1
The set-up
Impure water (e.g. salt water) is heated in a flask. A thermometer measures the temperature of the steam.
2
Boiling
The water boils at 100 °C and turns into steam. Salt and other impurities do not turn into steam — they stay in the flask. Microorganisms are killed by the heat.
3
The condenser
Cold water flows in the outer tube of the condenser. Where should the cold water go in?
4
Condensation
The steam is cooled in the condenser and condenses into liquid water. The pure water collected is called distilled water.
5
Water in at the bottom — why?
Cold water goes in at the bottom and out at the top. Then the condenser is always completely full of cold water, so it cools the steam better.

Distilled water has no soluble impurities, insoluble impurities or microorganisms.
